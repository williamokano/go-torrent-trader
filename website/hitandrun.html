Enforcement

Hit-and-run

A hit-and-run is a torrent someone downloaded and then stopped seeding before giving anything back. Tracking it is off by default. This page describes exactly what the system does once it is on — what opens an obligation, what closes one, and what happens if obligations pile up — so an operator can decide whether the defaults suit their site and a member can read the same rules the daemon actually applies.

Off by default, and never retroactive. Nothing is tracked until an operator turns hnr_enabled on, and even then tracking starts from that moment forward. Torrents already downloaded before the switch was flipped are never given an obligation after the fact.

Step one

What opens an obligation

One thing, and only one thing: finishing a download. The moment a client reports a torrent complete, a record opens against that member for that torrent. Nothing else does it — clicking download, grabbing the .torrent file, starting a download and abandoning it, or a client that announces completed while bytes are still outstanding all leave no trace. A partial download owes nothing here.

This is deliberately narrower than most trackers. TorrentLeech, for instance, opens a hit-and-run on anything downloaded past 10% and requires the member to seed that fragment back to a 1:1 ratio. We do not track partials at all.

Three further cases never open an obligation:

Case Why
Exempt torrent Staff can flag any torrent exempt by hand, and an automatic pass exempts torrents by seeder count (well enough seeded that one member leaving does not matter) or by size (too small for the bookkeeping to be worth it). Flagging a torrent exempt also waives obligations already open against it.
Exempt class Hit-and-run rules are set per member class, and a class with no rule is not tracked. This is how "VIP does not get hit-and-runs" is expressed. Donors can be exempted separately with hnr_exempt_donors.
An old snatch re-announcing A client re-checking or re-adding a torrent finished long ago announces as if it had just completed. If the snatch list already dates the completion more than an hour back, no fresh obligation opens.

Step two

How an obligation is satisfied

Two ways, and meeting either closes the record for good: seed the torrent for the required number of hours, or upload enough of it to reach the required ratio. The class rules set both numbers. The pre-filled values when an operator first puts a class on the ladder are 96 hours or a 1:1 ratio — the same requirement TorrentLeech publishes.

The ratio is measured against the torrent's real size, not against what was counted toward the member's ratio. A freeleech torrent still has to be seeded back like any other; the discount applies to ratio accounting, not to the seeding obligation.

Seed time accrues from announces. A gap between two seeding announces longer than hnr_seed_credit_cap_minutes earns no credit for that gap, so time can only be banked by actually staying connected — a client that reappears for one announce a day cannot stitch those into four days of seeding.

Step three

When a record becomes a hit-and-run

An open record is not yet a hit-and-run. It becomes one when the member stops seeding with the requirement unmet — specifically, when no seeding announce has arrived for longer than the class's inactivity grace (48 hours in the pre-filled rule). A class can also set a hard deadline to satisfy the requirement, though the pre-filled value is no deadline at all.

It stops being one the instant seeding resumes. A single seeding announce moves the record back to open, and it will close normally once the time or ratio requirement is met. Nothing has to be appealed and no staff member has to intervene.

Members can see their own open obligations, what each one still needs, and what it would cost to clear, on their hit-and-run page. The count is evaluated live there rather than read from the last daemon sweep, so it is never stale.

Step four

What happens if they pile up

Penalties run on a ladder. Each rung has a number of unresolved hit-and-runs that must be reached and a length of time the member must have spent on the previous rung before it can be entered. A member climbs at most one rung per daemon sweep, however far past the thresholds their count has jumped — but they fall as far as their count warrants in one step. Improving is never rationed.

How many unresolved hit-and-runs it takes to reach a penalty rung is one setting, hnr_penalty_threshold, rather than a figure repeated on every rung. Changing it moves the whole ladder together. It ships at 50, calibrated against TorrentLeech's published rules, and the ladder below stops one rung short of theirs at every point:

Rung Needs What happens
1 1 hit-and-run A reminder, and nothing else. No penalty attaches to this rung — a member can sit here indefinitely with one forgotten torrent and never hear about it again.
2 the threshold (50) A notice that the account has crossed the threshold at which warnings begin, and that it has five days to drop back below it. This rung is what starts the clock.
3 the threshold, held 5 days A formal warning.
4 the threshold, held a further 30 days A second formal warning.
5 the threshold, held a further 30 days Downloading is suspended for 14 days. Forum, chat and everything else are untouched — they have nothing to do with seeding.
6 the threshold, held a further 30 days The account is disabled.

So at the shipped threshold the earliest an account can be disabled by this system is roughly 95 days of continuously holding 50 or more unresolved hit-and-runs, through two warnings and a download suspension. Below the threshold the ladder never leaves rung one — a member with one forgotten torrent gets a reminder and is otherwise left alone.

Falling back below a threshold reverses everything automatically: the member drops straight to the rung matching their live count, and any download suspension the ladder applied is lifted in the same sweep. A ban is the exception — an account disabled at rung six is re-enabled by staff, not by the daemon. Warnings expire on their own after hnr_warning_expiry_days (30 by default), which clears the warned flag once nothing else is outstanding.

Every number above is editable on the Hit-and-Run admin page — the threshold, dwell times, actions, which privileges a restrict rung suspends, and the message each one sends. Rungs can be added and removed. 50 is calibrated against one of the largest private trackers in existence, so a site with a few hundred members will want it far lower; that is one number to change, at the top of the penalty ladder section. A rung can also pin its own figure and ignore the threshold entirely, which is how the opening reminder stays at one obligation however the rest of the ladder is tuned.

The other way out

Clearing an obligation with points

A member who genuinely cannot seed a torrent back — the data is gone, the drive died, nobody is left in the swarm — can pay an obligation off with bonus points instead. The record closes and stops counting toward the ladder.

The price is set per class or site-wide, in one of two modes. Fixed pricing charges a flat amount plus a rate per GiB of the torrent's size. Deficit pricing charges per GiB of upload still needed to reach the required ratio, so somebody who seeded most of the way back pays proportionally less than somebody who seeded nothing. Every payment goes through the same bonus ledger as every other movement, so it is auditable afterwards.

Housekeeping

How it runs

A daemon sweeps once an hour. It marks records satisfied or breached, applies the automatic torrent exemptions, walks every affected member up or down the ladder, and purges resolved records older than hnr_retention_days. Every sweep writes a log row — scanned, breached, satisfied, escalated, decayed, purged — visible on the admin page, which can also force a sweep on demand. Running two at once is safe; the second waits and a third is dropped.

The settings named on this page are documented in full, with their defaults, on the Configure page.
